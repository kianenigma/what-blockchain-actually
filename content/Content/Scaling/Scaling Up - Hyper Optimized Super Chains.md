The first way to improve the scalability of a blockchain network is not to touch the architecture explained in [[Introduction - Why Scaling Matters]], but to add a combination of hardware and software optimizations to scale the system. [Solana](https://solana.com/pt/news/8-innovations-that-make-solana-the-first-web-scale-blockchain) is perhaps the best example of such an approach. This method evidently works to an extent, as seen in the high transaction rates of Solana, but it also poses some challenges:
- The [hardware requirements](https://docs.solanalabs.com/operations/requirements) to run a validator are increasingly high.
- At the end of the day, this method is a form of vertical scaling, and there are questions about whether it will grow indefinitely or not.

All of the properties of vertical scaling discussed in [[Introduction - Why Scaling Matters#Vertical and Horizontal Scaling]] apply to this approach.
