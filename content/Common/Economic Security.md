The amount of tokens that a blockchain protocol locks, and potentially holds subject to [[Slashing]], as a punishment of [[Validator]]s who misbehave.

In other words, it is the cost that it would take to bribe or control the majority of the [[Validator]]s of a blockchain network. If one can successfully do this, they can essentially control that network. The higher this number is, the more secure a protocol is. 

Economic security is the main property that delivers **verifiable execution**, one of the pillars of [[Trustless]] blockchain networks, and plays a key role in other properties too. We trust that the blockchain executes its [[STF]] correctly, because we know validators of a said blockchain have billions of dollars of tokens at stake, and game theory informs us that they will not risk an incorrect execution of the [[STF]] knowing they will get slashed.

As the economic security of a network increases, the the confidence in its [[Trustless]]-ness increases.
